## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** StreamLine
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** Bottom line: if StreamLine does nothing for 12 months, it keeps its lead on library size and loses its strategic position. It’s still the biggest service, but it becomes the cheap utility people pay for and cancel easily, while the curated services take its most valuable viewers and its reputation as the place to find things to watch.

How the decline plays out 
The best customers leave first. The heavy viewers who care about discovery are the ones churning, and they’re also the highest-LTV, least price-sensitive, most influential (they recommend shows and their activity drives the recommendations). Losing them hurts far more than the subscriber count shows.
The subscriber base gets worse. Fewer of those viewers remain and more casual, price-sensitive ones stay. Average revenue per user flattens because price rises become risky, so the business slides toward discounts and ad tiers to hold the headline number.
The library becomes a cost trap. Content costs are fixed and keep growing, but engagement is flat. Cost per hour watched rises every quarter. “More is better” becomes “more is more expensive.”
Discovery gets worse. Less engagement from serious viewers means weaker signals for recommendations, so browsing feels like scrolling through a warehouse. That pushes out more of the remaining serious viewers.
Competitors pull ahead. Curated services win the cultural conversation, critical prestige, and more and more the licensors and talent who want their work presented well, not buried. Their brand advantage compounds while StreamLine’s volume advantage is easy to copy.

Rough 12-month path

Q1–Q2: Headline subscribers look stable because gross adds hide the churn. Engagement per subscriber keeps dropping. Leadership reads “we’re fine.”
Q3: Net additions turn negative as churn passes acquisition. Acquisition cost rises because StreamLine is now paying to replace customers, not to grow.
Q4: Margins get squeezed by content spend against flat revenue. The story shifts from “largest library” to “commoditised aggregator.” Rebuilding the premium position now costs more and takes longer than it would have at month 0.

The strategic point: the volume lead is what hides the crisis. Every dashboard that tracks title count or total subscribers will look acceptable until about Q3, and by then the high-value viewers are gone. The danger isn’t a collapse in 12 months. It’s that StreamLine leaves the year locked into competing on price and scale against companies with bigger balance sheets (the big tech bundles), while the niche services own the premium end. Its position gets squeezed from both sides.

“Doing nothing isn’t neutral. It’s a decision to become a commodity.” That sets up Spotlight as a way to defend the business’s most valuable viewers, not a nice extra.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The moment of misery: it’s 9pm, a serious viewer opens StreamLine wanting something good, scrolls for 15–20 minutes through rows that all look the same, doesn’t trust any of them, then picks up their phone. At that point StreamLine has become a catalogue they search with another tool open. (These are assumed research findings, based on typical discovery behaviour.)

What they do instead

Workaround	What they’re actually after	What it says StreamLine lacks
Letterboxd / IMDb / Rotten Tomatoes on a second screen	Proof a title is worth 2 hours	Trustworthy quality signals. Thumbnails and “98% match” aren’t credible to these viewers
Googling “best films on StreamLine right now” / Reddit threads	Someone with taste to filter 15,000 titles	Human curation. Algorithm rows feel like inventory, not a recommendation
JustWatch / Reelgood	“Where can I watch the film I already want?”	A reason to start on StreamLine. They decide elsewhere and StreamLine becomes the pipe
Personal watchlists in Notes, spreadsheets or Letterboxd lists	Saving recommendations from friends, critics, festivals	A watchlist worth keeping. The native “My List” is a dumping ground with no notes, context or sharing
YouTube video essays, critic newsletters, podcasts	Context: why this film matters, what to watch next	Editorial depth around a title
Group chats / asking a friend	A recommendation from someone they trust	Social and identity signals (“people with your taste loved this”)
MUBI, Criterion Channel, niche services	All of the above in one place	This is the churn path. The workaround becomes the replacement

The pattern: none of these viewers is short of content. They’re short of confidence. They’re spending time and attention outside StreamLine just to work out what’s worth watching inside it. Each workaround moves a habit somewhere else, and once the habit lives elsewhere, the subscription is easy to cancel.

What this means for Spotlight: each workaround maps to a feature to bring back in-house:

Second-screen checking → visible credibility signals (critic picks, festival credentials, named curators)
Reddit / Google → human-led collections with a point of view, not genre rows
Notes / spreadsheets → a personal film diary or watchlist worth maintaining
Video essays → context layers (short intros, “why this matters”, watch-next pathways)
Group chats → taste-based social proof
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** StreamLine’s most valuable viewers have 15,000 titles and no confidence in any of them, so they’re doing their discovery on Letterboxd, Reddit and rival apps, and every month we treat library size as a strategy we lose more of our highest-value subscribers to services that tell them what’s worth watching.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** Value proposition: StreamLine Spotlight turns the biggest library in streaming into the most trusted place to find great cinema: human-curated, credible picks that let serious viewers decide in minutes, without leaving the app.

The new value

For the viewer, confidence instead of choice:

Less effort: a short, curated set of films picked by named curators replaces the 20-minute scroll
Trust: critic, festival and curator credentials sit on the title, so there’s no second screen
Context: why a film matters and what to watch next, so each watch builds taste rather than just filling an evening
Ownership: a watchlist or film diary worth keeping, which moves the habit back into StreamLine

For the business, depth from the scale it already has:

Retention of the highest-LTV cohort, the viewers currently being lost to niche services
Better return on existing content spend: Spotlight brings back long-tail titles StreamLine already pays for. It’s a discovery layer, not a new acquisition budget
Pricing power: a premium tier or add-on gives a reason to pay more, not just a reason to stay
Brand repositioning, from “the warehouse” to “the authority,” without giving up mass-market reach

Why now

The window is closing. Churn is still at the early stage where headline subscribers look stable. By roughly Q3 net adds turn negative, and winning lapsed viewers back costs far more than keeping them.
Competitors are building the habit. Every month a viewer spends on Letterboxd or MUBI, their discovery routine hardens somewhere else. Habits are much harder to win back than subscriptions.
The asset already exists. The 15,000 titles are a sunk cost that’s being underused. StreamLine is paying for a library its best customers can’t navigate, so every month without a discovery layer wastes content spend.
Low-risk entry point. Spotlight sits inside the existing app, so it can be piloted with one cohort, measured against browse-to-play time and churn, and scaled or killed quickly. It doesn’t touch the mass-market experience.
